

                         How to Choose Your Own AI Tools  

    Looking back over the short six years from 2020 to 2026, the field of artificial intelligence has undergone a paradigm shift comparable to the Industrial Revolution. In 2020, the release of GPT-3 was just a celebration within a small circle of geeks and researchers; by the end of 2022, ChatGPT emerged, completely igniting global AI enthusiasm; and now in 2026, AI has evolved from being a "magic cube in a chat box" to a "digital worker capable of independently planning and executing tasks."    
    How many AI websites and apps exist globally? How is AI software classified? What are the top 30 AI websites and apps worldwide? With so many rapidly evolving AI tools, how should ordinary people and even companies choose? This is a fundamental question that must be clarified before fully entering the AI era. This article will provide a comprehensive AI tool guide from four dimensions: market size, classification system, selection methodology, and emerging trends.

1. Global AI Application Market: From Explosive Growth to Deep-Water Consolidation             Understanding the variety of AI tools starts with grasping the market size. According to data from QuestMobile and several top investment banks, the global AI market experienced explosive growth from 2020 to 2026. 
    1.1 The 'Nuclear Explosion' of App Numbers and Retention： In 2020, there were less than a hundred large-scale generative AI applications worldwide; by the second half of 2025, 205 new AI apps were launched (81.5% mobile web apps, 10.7% PC web apps, 7.8% native apps). Currently, there are thousands of active AI applications globally (including websites, apps, API services, and various plugins), forming a full-stack ecosystem from foundational large models to vertical scenario apps. By 2026, the market had left behind the 'throw-up-a-webpage-and-get-funded' wild era, with many peripheral apps lacking core barriers quietly exiting, and market share rapidly consolidating at the top. 
    1.2 Capital and Spending Frenzy： In 2025, global spending on generative AI is expected to reach $644 billion, a year-on-year increase of 76.4%; the global AI market has reached $391 billion. By 2030, the market is expected to surpass $1.81 trillion, with a compound annual growth rate of 35.9%, exceeding the growth slope seen during the cloud computing and mobile app booms. 
    1.3 The New Turning Point in 2026: From 'Dialogue' to 'Action' From 2020's 'discriminative AI dominance' (like facial recognition and recommendation algorithms), to 2023's 'generative AI frenzy' (writing articles, creating images), by 2026 the biggest market variable is the rise of AI Agents. Agent applications represented by Manus and OpenClaw are opening entirely new tracks, with AI no longer just being a 'question-answering encyclopedia', but becoming 'super assistants' that can automatically book flights, write and test code, and even operate computers to complete end-to-end tasks.

3. Overcoming Choice Fatigue: The 'Needs-Scenario-Ecosystem' Three-Dimensional Selection Method 
    When faced with a huge number of tools, ordinary users can easily fall into 'AI tool fatigue'—downloading various apps every day, only to find that none of them get used. Remember one golden rule: there is no perfect tool, only the most suitable one. It is recommended to filter through the following three-step method: 
    Step 1: The Three Soul Questions—clarify the core needs (most crucial) 
    What to do? Handle boring text and spreadsheets? Create stunning PPTs? Or produce product promo videos? Who is it for? Personal exploration and entertainment? Team collaboration? Or enterprise-level private deployment (involving data compliance)? What scenario? Do you need absolutely precise professional scenarios (like legal documents), or creative scenarios for brainstorming and divergent thinking?
    Step 2: Measure with a ruler – Assess the 5 core indicators 
    Effectiveness (Is it accurate?): Can the results be used straight away? Does the text have severe 'hallucinations'? Do images require a lot of post-editing? 
    Usability (Is it easy to use?): Is the interface intuitive? Can anyone pick it up easily or do you need to learn complex 'prompt engineering'? 
    Cost (Is it expensive?): Can the free tier meet daily needs? Once you get dependent, is a $20 monthly subscription within budget? 
    Compatibility (Can it integrate?): Is it an isolated webpage or does it seamlessly plug into Word, Excel, Feishu or browsers? 
    Privacy and security (Is it trustworthy?): This will be a key concern for companies in 2025-2026. Will confidential data be used to train public cloud models? Sensitive operations should choose tools that support local deployment or sign strict privacy agreements (like DPA).
    Step 3: Take Your Seat - 2026 Practical Tool Selection Suggestions
    For high-frequency needs, here are tool recommendations based on the latest developments:
    Daily chat/Q&A: Choose Doubao, ChatGPT, Kimi. In a Chinese context, Doubao and Kimi offer generous free quotas and are very user-friendly; ChatGPT still tops in overall intelligence.
    Professional writing/long reports: Choose Kimi (excellent 2 million character context), DeepSeek (amazing deep reasoning ability, very low cost), Claude (most 'human-like' writing, extremely logical).
    AI search/data lookup: Choose Perplexity, Mita AI Search. They provide answers with source references, completely transforming the traditional search engine experience.
    Design/painting: Choose Midjourney (aesthetic ceiling), SeaArt/Jimeng (friendly with Chinese prompts, cost-effective).
    Video editing/generation: Choose Keling AI (leading globally in understanding physical motion), Wink (very easy to use on mobile).Office/PPT: Choose WPS AI, Gamma. No design skills needed, import an outline to generate beautiful presentations.

4. Special Considerations for Team and Enterprise-Level Selection 
    Choosing tools for a company or team isn’t just about 'buying a few memberships'. To truly integrate AI into employees with different professional backgrounds, at least five aspects need to be considered: 
    Multi-role adaptability: Programmers need Cursor, designers need Midjourney, operations need copywriting tools. Enterprise selection should choose platform-based AI (like Feishu AI, DingTalk AI) or services that provide a unified API gateway to integrate various vertical tools into the same workspace. 
    Knowledge base and memory isolation: Team collaboration requires AI to understand the company background. It must support building a 'private enterprise knowledge base (RAG)', and data and conversation memories for different departments (such as finance and marketing) must be strictly isolated. Seamless workflow integration: AI cannot be an isolated island. Can AI responses from the customer service department directly trigger the workflow in the ticket system? This is key to evaluating whether AI can generate real ROI. 
    Controllable cost model: Shift from monthly subscriptions to pay-per-token billing. Enterprises need to establish internal consumption monitoring dashboards to prevent employees from misuse causing out-of-control bills. 
    Compliance and data sovereignty: In the current geopolitical context, for sensitive enterprises, state-owned companies, and government institutions, some countries prefer domestically developed large model private deployments (like Volcano Ark, locally deployed DeepSeek open-source versions) and must not connect core data to foreign public cloud APIs.

Conclusion 
    The current global AI application market is at a historic critical point, shifting from 'a single tool to a new one' to an 'agent collaborative workflow.' 
    Faced with a flood of choices, our best approach should be: subtract first, then add. Don’t try to get everything. Start with the area you need most (like writing weekly reports, making PPTs, researching), pick one or two main tools, and use them until they become second nature; then, depending on the situation, bring in specialised auxiliary tools like building blocks, gradually creating your own 'super brain' external brain system. 
    In future competition, it won’t be AI itself that knocks you out, but those 'super individuals' who integrate AI into their workflows before you, hitting a dimensionality reduction strike. Choose the AI tool that fits you—now's the perfect time.
