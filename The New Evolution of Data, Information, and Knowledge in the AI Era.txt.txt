

                   The New Evolution of Data, Information, and Knowledge in the AI Era

      In the past, data, information, and knowledge used to flow in a one-way, linear chain centred on humans; but in the AI era, the lines between data, information, and knowledge are blurring, bringing in a new mix of integration, dynamics, and human-machine teamwork. Nowadays, especially with generative AI like large language models, the classic DIKW model (Data - Information - Knowledge - Wisdom) is being completely shaken up and reinvented.       Data, information, and knowledge haven't just changed in what they mean, they've also moved from a straight-line progression to popping up in a more coordinated way. This change isn't just about tech getting better, it's also a big shift in how we understand things. Here's a deeper look at what they now mean, their traits, and how they're evolving.

1. New Meaning: From 'Static Recording' to 'Dynamic Intelligent Components'
      1.1. Data: From 'Recording Objective Facts' to 'New Quality AI Production Factors' 
      Traditional DIKW models see data as discrete, objective raw signals. In the AI era, data has gained an unprecedented strategic role. 
      Factorisation of production: Data has become the fifth major production factor after land, labour, capital, and technology, and is now part of the production function. It isn¡¯t just a carrier of information anymore but a labour object that directly contributes to value creation.          Non-resource attributes: Unlike consumable resources like oil, data isn¡¯t competitive (it can be used by multiple parties), can be reused at zero marginal cost, and boosts returns to scale. While being used, data doesn¡¯t get used up but actually produces new data, creating a 'data-new data' growth cycle. 
      Computationalisation: In the smart economy, data, algorithms, and computing power form the core of 'ABCD' (Algorithm, Business scenarios, Computing, Data), acting as the 'fuel' for AI systems. 
      New meaning in the AI era: Data is AI's 'sensory input' and 'basis for existence.' Even more, synthetic data has appeared¡ªdata produced by AI itself. Data is no longer just a passive representation of the real world but a 'virtual experience' that machines can actively build.
      1.2. Information: From "structured data" to "generative semantic units" 
      The usual idea of information is "data given context," but in the AIGC era, information has taken on a new form: 
      Generative replacement: In the past, getting information meant searching and matching, but now large models can generate information instantly. Information isn't just stored statically anymore; it's generated dynamically depending on what you need. 
      Multimodal fusion: Information goes beyond just text, becoming a mix of text, images, video, and code. Models like CLIP help connect meaning across these different types.       Personalisation emerges: Information is moving from a "one-size-fits-all" approach to a more personalised "one person, one policy" style. AI can change how detailed and how information is shown in real time based on what the user knows. With tech like RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation), information isn't just something for humans to read; it's a "guide" that the model can use, acting as a language that links human intent with machine computing.
      1.3. Knowledge: Moving from "verified truth" to "probabilistic patterns and parametric memory" 
      Knowledge isn't just a human thing anymore; the way it's created and exists has totally changed. 
      Machine Knowledge: AIGC can create new knowledge through a process of "decompose-learn-reorganise-generate," which makes sense logically but might not come from human experience. This 'third source of knowledge' (between reason and faith) is about making tacit knowledge explicit. 
      Networked Authors: Old-school knowledge was made by individual authors, but in the AI era, it's more about teams¡ªprogrammers make algorithms, users give prompts, AI generates, creating a "human-machine teamwork" knowledge network. 
      Immediacy and Mobility: Knowledge is turning from "archival storage" to a fluid thing. Big models speed up learning by turning the slow "retrieve-read-internalise" process into real-time "ask-generate-check" interactions. 
      New knowledge in the AI era means knowledge is now billions of numbers (weights and biases) in neural nets. It's not clear propositions anymore but probability distributions and geometric distances in high-dimensional space. Machines don't 'understand' it, but they grasp the 'usage patterns' behind it (emergent capabilities).

2. New Features: Liquefaction, Recession, and Synthesis
      2.1. The 'Meltability' of Boundaries 
      In the past, in an article, the original numbers were data, paragraphs were the main idea of information, and the core idea was knowledge, with clear boundaries. But in AI's generative output, a single sentence can include data, information sharing, and knowledge reasoning all at once, all blended into an inseparable 'semantic fluid' by AI. 
      2.2. The 'Liquefaction' and 'Immediacy' of Existence
      In the past, knowledge was solid (written in books and stored in databases), and you just pulled it out when you needed it. Now, knowledge is liquid; it appears instantly. The same AI model, when asked questions by different people, can instantly 'condense' into different types of knowledge. Knowledge isn¡¯t just a static storage anymore, it¡¯s like a dynamic fountain. 
      2.3. The 'Black Box' and 'Implicit' 
      Side of Carriers Human knowledge comes out through language and writing, while AI¡¯s knowledge is tucked away in its parameters. Because it¡¯s 'only intuitive (not directly readable)', AI knowledge can¡¯t really be explained. We have systems packed with knowledge, but we can¡¯t just look up exactly what it knows like flipping through a dictionary.
      2.4. The "Synthesis" and "Emergence" of Production 
      Data and knowledge can be "created out of nothing" (like AI writing code or generating virtual cases to train medical models). Also, when the amount of data passes a certain threshold, the model can suddenly develop abilities never seen in the training data (like a large model suddenly learning to solve maths problems), which is the "emergence" of knowledge. 3. New Evolution: From 'Linear Chains' to 'Closed-Loop Flywheels'
      3.1. Flipping Evolution on Its Head: From 'Induction' to 'Deductive Generation' 
      Traditional evolution: data ¡ú information¡ú knowledge (bottom-up induction and extraction). 
      The new AI way: knowledge (model weights) ¡ú information (generated text) ¡ú data (user clicks, feedback logs). AI is basically going backwards, using its built-in 'tacit knowledge' to keep putting out 'explicit information' and 'synthetic data.' 
       3.2. The 'Lossless Compression Hypothesis' of Knowledge Acquisition 
       Ilya Sutskever (co-founder of OpenAI) once suggested that training big models is basically a 'lossless compression' of loads of internet data and human knowledge. The model doesn¡¯t just memorise stuff, it finds the most efficient patterns behind the data (like creating ZIP files for data). So, the evolutionary process goes: compression (training) ¡ú decompression (reasoning/generation).
      3.3. The evolutionary "bootstrap" and closed-loop flywheel 
      Traditional knowledge evolution has relied on the slow build-up of human scientists and scholars. Now, there¡¯s a closed-loop flywheel of "data hunger": AI-generated content ¡ú enriching the internet data pool ¡ú becoming training material for the next generation of AI ¡ú creating even stronger AI. 
      (Note: This also brings the risk of a "model crash"¡ªif AI keeps using its own generated data, it could lead to knowledge degradation, which is a new challenge in evolution.)
      3.4. Production Paradigm: The Leap from "Auxiliary" to "Dominating" 
      Content production has gone through three major shifts: 
      PGC Era (Professional Development): Expensive, slow, knowledge mainly held by professionals. 
      UGC Era (User-Generated): Quick but lower quality, knowledge is open but scattered. 
      AIGC Era (AI Generation): Combines high quality, speed, and personalisation, moving into a content production world where AI leads and humans assist. 
      By 2026, 78% of content creators are expected to use AI tools, with AI-made content making up 35%.
      3.5. Economic Form: The Multiplier Effect of Data Elements 
      Data elements boost productivity in three ways: 
      Direct empowerment: Making production more efficient with tools like smart factories and automated decision-making. 
      Multiplier effect: Working closely with traditional resources like labour and capital to create more value. 
      Restructuring of production relations: Shifting ownership structures (where control of data becomes key) and giving rise to new job roles like prompt engineers and AI trainers.
      This transformation has led to new high-quality productive forces¡ªadvanced new forces focused on data, featuring human-machine collaboration, and marked by advanced technology, high efficiency, and top quality. 
      3.6. Epistemological Philosophy: The Crisis of Subjectivity in Knowledge
      The AI era has brought up some deep questions about knowledge: machine learning gives us new ways to understand the world, but it also challenges the idea that humans are the only ones who directly know things. 
      The Crisis of Knowledge Determinism: The 'hallucination' issue in AIGC shows that generative knowledge is uncertain¡ªknowledge isn¡¯t just a reflection of the real world anymore, it¡¯s a statistical result from probabilistic models. 
      Lack of Wisdom: Even though AI can turn data into information and then into knowledge, the wisdom bit¡ªlike making value judgements, ethical decisions, and having aesthetic experiences¡ªis still really hard for machines to copy. 
      3.7. DIKW¡¯s Ultimate Goal Shift: from chasing 'wisdom' to going after 'alignment'.
      In the past, humans climbed the DIKW pyramid, aiming to gain 'wisdom'¡ªthat is, values, judgment, and a sense of morality. 
      Now, AI has outstripped humans in terms of data and knowledge, but it still lacks intelligence. So, the top task for DIKW evolution in the AI era isn¡¯t about giving machines more knowledge, but about 'alignment'¡ªfiguring out how to safely and ethically utilise the machine's vast probabilistic knowledge. Alignment has become the new peak of evolution, replacing 'intelligence' in the AI era. 

4. Deep Challenges: Beyond the Technical Dimension
      The shift from data to information to knowledge in the AI age comes with three main risks of alienation: 
      4.1. Losing our autonomy because of relying on AI: When AI handles everything from collecting data to creating knowledge, people might stop asking questions since computers give easy ready-made answers. This could make us less curious and less engaged with discovery. 
      4.2. Knowledge becoming the same everywhere and local knowledge fading: AIGC, built on huge datasets, might just keep pushing mainstream knowledge, which could bury niche, local ways of knowing. The algorithm¡¯s bias towards the mainstream could make us lose cultural variety. 
      4.3. Unequal data power: As data becomes a key resource, it might lead to more concentration of wealth and control. Companies that run data systems and algorithm platforms could end up having massive influence over society.

5. Conclusion 
      The evolution of data, information and knowledge in the AI era is basically a revolution in cognitive infrastructure. The traditional DIKW pyramid is being transformed into a network-like collaborative ecosystem¡ªdata is the flowing blood, information is the woven neural network, and knowledge is the emerging form of intelligence. 
      The real competitive edge in the future won¡¯t be who has more knowledge (because AI can whip it up instantly), but who¡¯s better at asking the right questions, designing cognitive frameworks and keeping critical thinking alive in human-machine collaboration. As the DIKW model points out: wisdom always stays with the individual and you need to get the audience¡¯s personal background to share it effectively. In a world of rapid tech advances, keeping the uniqueness and subjectivity of human intelligence might be our most basic spiritual independence and survival right in the age of AI.
      In the AI era, data is food, information is a spell, and knowledge is magic (parameters). 
      We don't have to painstakingly pan for gold from the sand (extracting knowledge from data) like we used to. AI melts gold into its blood; as long as we give the right spell (prompt/message), it can conjure gold anytime, anywhere. Getting this shift from 'physical' to 'functional' is key to navigating the AI era.
